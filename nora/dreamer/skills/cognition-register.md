## Choose the record that carries the situation

Memory preserves what was stated. The notebook preserves what people are actually carrying forward: a note for an accepted plan with an unsettled corner, an event for a dated gathering, and a memo for a settled thing the group will need again. A casual wish, conditional suggestion or unanswered proposal has not become a plan. A person's fact belongs to memory; an explicit request to retain settled group information is honored through the appropriate admitted write. A title identifies a matter; the body carries its value.

## Continue the same matter

Read the existing notes, memos and this room's events before adding or revising. The same arrangement may have another title, while shared people, dates or places alone do not make two arrangements identical. Update the held record when supported evidence changes its content, preserving unchanged lines and their citations; repetition or rewording alone earns no revision. A cancelled plan moves its line to Ruled out with the call-off as the reason and its event to cancelled. If nothing under Decided and nothing under Open remains, the body is refused: leave the note as it is until its status is settled through the admitted close. A moved plan stays open at its new date. An empty body is not a way to retire a record.

## Make each line re-derivable

Write the arrangement rather than a recap of who spoke. A line names its people and concrete details as stated and cites the rows that establish them. Each line keeps its existing citations when an update restates the whole body. A reason nobody gave stays `no reason given`; an Owed line needs evidence that the person took it on, while a request they never answered remains Open. An anchor_date is when the matter happens, grounded in the supplied clock and row time, including their weekdays; anchor_entities distinguish separate arrangements on the same day. A relative day in a note line becomes its grounded date. A closing_condition names the evidence or event that would settle the question.

Event modes describe how you know: told for what a person stated (including a relative date resolved from their words), deduced for what follows from held evidence, abduced for a tentative explanation. An abduced value reaches people as a question, and agreement to your guess does not change its provenance. Notes contain what was said; inferred event values do not become attributed note lines.

## Retire for a reason

Retire as settled when cited evidence answers the question, keeping a memo first if the settled result remains useful. A passed date earns anchor_passed only when nothing remains open. Silence supplies no answer, and a plan moved to another date has not ended. When you cannot tell, leave them apart: two entries cost an extra line, while two distinct questions folded into one lose a question. Merge only when the same resolution settles both questions, preserving every distinct open line and its citations. When evidence cannot settle identity or closure, leave the questions apart and open.

## Earn the later interruption

A reminder earns its time when an approaching unresolved matter makes a word useful to someone who can act. Read its record and the admitted context about the people so the timing fits the situation. A settled matter, a point already raised or a repetition of the notebook supplies no new reason. Keep one reminder for the same matter, revise it when the situation changes and choose no reminder when nothing warrants interrupting the room.
