# Platform

A platform is where people talk to Nora: Signal, Discord, WhatsApp and
Instagram messaging. Each has a **bridge**, an independent process that owns the
platform account and session. Nora's app never speaks a native protocol. One
platform failing, restarting or logging out cannot take the others, or the app,
with it.

A bridge produces [items](../cognition/observation.md) and performs
[acts](../cognition/acts.md). It decides no meaning: no model runs in a bridge.

## Parts

| part | responsibility |
|---|---|
| **bridge** (one per platform) | the account session; normalising native events once; admission before capture; the durable journal; media custody; synchronous sends, reactions and reads |
| **queue** | one ordered stream of captured events per platform account, with pending-delivery state, persisted across app restarts |
| **app side** | one client for every bridge; one stream reader per stream; the ingest and engagement work that follows |
| **action record** | each outbound effect's identity, request, state and platform reference |

Platform differences stop at the bridge: how an account is identified, which
native events exist, how media is held, what a send can express. Everything
past the platform contract is the same for every platform, so a new platform
adds a bridge and its fixtures, never another reply workflow.

## Inbound

1. **Admission before capture.** The bridge reads an admission projection,
   generated by the same code the app uses to judge policy and identity and
   limited to enabled rooms, admitted direct senders, the account's own
   aliases and deterministic trigger facts. A room outside it produces no
   journal entry, no media and no queued work. No credential or full identity
   file is mounted into a bridge. A bridge without a valid projection captures
   nothing; a reload that fails keeps the last good projection and reports it
   stale.
2. **Normalise once.** A native event becomes one typed event: a message with
   its mentions, quote facts and media, an edit, a deletion, a reaction, a
   native observation or a summons. The app rehydrates this value and never
   parses the raw text again. Its identity is a stable key over platform,
   account, conversation, kind and the upstream message, revision or
   transition, so a replay is recognised and a body hash is never the
   identity.
3. **Capture commit point.** The bridge appends the event to its journal and
   syncs it before anything else. The journal is a retry spool for the
   queue, not a second record. If it cannot hold the event, the bridge stops
   receiving from the platform rather than dropping the oldest input and
   continuing as though capture were complete.
4. **Enqueue and receipt.** The journal publishes to the queue in order.
   A queued receipt, such as a reaction, means *queued* and nothing more. It is
   sent only for an addressed event from an admitted sender, once the event is
   in the queue, and never for a room or sender the policy denies.
5. **Ownership until durable.** The app keeps a queue entry pending until the
   event's ingest and turn are finished, or each piece of deferred work has a
   durable owner. A swallowed failure is a typed outcome (done, intentionally
   skipped, retryable, retained, uncertain), never a silent success. Policy
   and identity are checked again when the backlog is consumed and before every
   effect; a revoked grant cancels the work.

Events from different conversations run in parallel. Within a conversation,
ingest keeps arrival order, so an edit cannot be recorded before the message it
edits, while recording later input never waits for a model turn in progress.

**Limits.** The queue guarantees continuity across app stops and replacements.
It cannot recover what the platform delivered before the bridge captured it, a
destroyed disk, or exactly-once effects a destination cannot provide.

## Outbound

Every send, reaction and edit is an [act](../cognition/acts.md) with a stable
identity reserved before the platform call. The bridge returns one of
*confirmed*, *rejected* (definitely not submitted, with whether a retry may
help), *unsupported* or *uncertain*. An uncertain act is never resent; it is
read back through its identity where the platform allows, and is otherwise
unknown. A work-bound action is fenced by its owner's generation, so a reclaimed
lane cannot repeat an effect under a stale claim.

- Targets are platform-qualified; a target whose platform does not match the
  bridge is refused before any effect.
- Quote and reaction references are opaque and typed per platform. One
  platform's handle is never derived from another's.
- A reply that spans several native posts keeps every confirmed handle in send
  order, and any of them resolves to the same recorded message. A rejected or
  uncertain delivery mints no handle and claims no count.
- A decoration a platform cannot show degrades to plain text; unsupported media
  or a reaction a platform cannot carry fails visibly and never fakes success.
- Roster, channel and presence reads carry their observation time. A roster
  that cannot be read in full is unavailable, never a smaller list.

## Media

Admitted media is held in the bridge's custody before its event is journaled,
so the event names the bytes or the explicit reason they are unavailable. The
app copies the bytes into its own store and checks their hash before
anything depends on them. The bridge releases its copy only after that copy and
the work that needs it are settled. Outbound bytes are held the same way until
the send is settled. How custody and reading work is the media bundle's
subject; this bundle owns only the handoff.

## Trust boundary

Bridges and the app talk over a private network with a shared bearer
credential; no bridge publishes a port. A bridge sees the queue, its own
session state and its own journal, never the app's database or model
configuration, and the app never holds a platform credential.

## Why this shape

- **Independent bridges** keep a release of the app from dropping the
  platform's session or its connection to a voice channel.
- **A durable journal and an ordered queue** make the loss window explicit:
  before capture, nothing is promised; after it, nothing is dropped.
- **One contract** keeps platform code out of cognition and makes every
  platform's failure look the same to it.
