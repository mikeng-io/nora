# Media interaction

## One turn, several distinct acts

A tag can produce more than one message when the turn contains genuinely
different acts: an answer, a question to another person, a reaction or a media
attachment. The target remains the tagging conversation; multiplicity never
becomes a new destination or an unbounded stream.

The reply mechanism must make stopping possible. The first response is
required, then the model may stop when the work is done. Per-message budget,
audit, disclosure and delivery guards apply independently, while the tag's
lifecycle resolves after the final act. Message count is a backstop, not the
stopping mechanism.

A reaction GIF and an explanatory text can therefore be separate messages
rather than a forced caption. Reading tone and recipient choice remain turn
judgements under the same room and disclosure rules.

## Shared media is for her, not an inventory to relay

A person sharing media already knows what it shows. The transcript and read
result supply what Nora needs to notice, not a list to translate back to them.
A request to describe gets the salient detail; a practical question gets the
answer that matters, such as the error, price or place.

A tool result says what it is for and what its evidence means. A vision
description is a model's reading, not fact to vouch for; a caption is the
poster's words; a cover is only a still, not the whole clip. Those roles remain
visible in the reply and prevent one successful but partial read from becoming
the whole answer.

A linked post or reel points to the item itself. Its cover is not offered as a
second, easier read. A story's cover can remain because a story link reads the
current story rather than necessarily the shared frame. An invalid or missing
link leaves the cover as the only faithful content.

Long captions are excerpts in the transcript and complete in the source and
post read. The excerpt visibly stops so the model does not mistake it for the
whole post. A generic web or media fetch cannot divert an Instagram page link
around the social reader and its asked-turn rule.

## Discovery and follow-up

Social discovery returns candidates and references; media reading returns what
an item shows. They are separate calls because their costs and meanings differ.
A profile or search reads no media, while a post or story can use the same
media reader with its own bounded slots and per-item outcomes.

When a further look would answer a different question than the first result,
the model may make it explicitly. Unread and not-read-here states identify what
remains available instead of prompting a blanket retry of every failed item.
