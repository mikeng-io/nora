# Profiles and disputes

## A bounded synthesis

A profile is Nora's current, revisable understanding of a person, room, unit,
another agent or herself. It is one account per entity per unit, with three
parts:

- **Facts** say who or what the entity is: names, roles, relationships, stable
  attributes and instructions. Authored names and account mappings render from
  their human owner and are never model-written.
- **Traits** describe supported recurring interests, habits, capabilities,
  preferences, dispositions, communication, values, social roles and continuing
  aims. A single occasion remains a reading or affair, not a durable trait.
- **Guesses** propose why a pattern occurs. Each carries a falsifiable
  prediction and window; an independent verdict confirms, refutes, expires or
  leaves it unknown. Even a confirmed explanation remains a hypothesis.

Facts and traits are different from guesses, and a profile is different from
the graph's full set of source statements. Facts may render from current
relations instead of maintaining a competing copy. Trait support is computed
from distinct underlying occasions, not message count, repeated indexing or the
writer's confidence.

Collective profiles describe observed collective practice, purpose and norms,
not an average of private member traits. Nora's profile describes how she comes
across through acts and responses; Lessons describe how she should act. Neither
can be derived from her earlier prose alone.

## Accuracy and precedence

Direct statements by the subject, statements by others, deduction and induction
retain their different attribution and precedence. A subject's instruction
about themselves comes only from that subject. Conflicting lines remain
inspectable, with the lower-precedence line outranked rather than silently
erased.

Typed values preserve units, conditions, effective time and uncertainty. An
unknown attribute has no invented default, and a temporary plan does not become
a stable fact. If the supported qualification cannot be represented, the source
remains a reading rather than becoming a falsely precise typed value.

Depicting or sharing something does not establish ownership, residence,
expertise or liking, and incidental activity — a borrowed car, a friend's dog
walked once — establishes no durable identity. Copies of the same bytes or the
same occasion add no support, and another agent's output is never evidence
about the person who runs it.

Location and other sensitive material follow the same accuracy rules as other
claims: supported named places and patterns may be stored without raw
coordinates or traces. This does not widen room permission. Existing disclosure
and location guards apply when a profile renders or is retrieved, and public
enrichment never includes private identity.

## Disputes and removal

Every line Nora holds about a person is visible to that person on the portal.
Anyone may dispute a line. The dispute is a Record item with its author and
evidence, and it wakes reconsideration.

A dispute or forget request from the line's subject hides that line immediately
from rendering and retrieval. A later revision can render only if it no longer
makes the disputed claim. Another person's dispute marks the line as awaiting
weighing; it remains visibly disputed until the writer keeps, revises or retires
it against who disputed, the evidence on each side and their times.

A deleted source message leaves the index and cannot be quoted as current
content. Judgements derived from it remain with their revision history and a
deleted-source citation; there is no automatic cascade that hides every
downstream judgement. The subject's direct hide is the deliberate exception,
not a general retraction rule.

## Revision and decay

The writer reads the held profile before revising it and changes only what the
new evidence warrants. Recurring traits need distinct supporting occasions and
lose authority when they are no longer re-evidenced. Counterexamples and
conditions remain attached rather than being removed to preserve a tidy card.

An independent verdict, not the writer, decides a guess's prediction. Assent to
Nora's own guess does not confirm it. Explanation and later verdict remain in
history even when a different factual conclusion is subsequently warranted.

A successful profile write is not proof of a closed learning loop. Later turns
must read the persisted account under their normal scope, and measured journeys
must show supported retrieval and correction rather than a growing collection
of lines.
