# Character

## One account per entity

Character and culture are the same kind of understanding at different scopes.
A room account describes collective practice; a unit account describes the
circle across its rooms; a person account describes that person within the
unit; Nora's account describes how she comes across there. Each has one keyed
revision chain and one owning session.

The evidence query differs by entity, not the execution model. Room and person
sessions read human messages and relevant derived records. A unit session reads
its rooms' cited cultures. Nora's session reads closed response episodes, not
bare sends. Presence and Standing are not profile evidence, and a person
account is never copied from a narrower room scope into the whole unit.

Evidence wakes the Dreamer scheduler when enough new material has accumulated
or new material has waited long enough. Quiet entities do not wake merely
because a clock passed. Code counts evidence, selects the key, supplies the
previous account and validates writes; the model investigates and judges what
the account should now say.

## Synthesis, not continuation

An account states the few traits or patterns that remain useful beyond the
occasions that taught them. It is re-synthesised on each revision: the previous
account is evidence to keep, merge or reject, not text to extend. A single
exchange, quotation, message number or recent event is not a durable trait.

Citations bind a revision to records or response episodes actually shown to its
session. Memory is an optional investigative tool, never a prefetched answer on
which the account depends. Nora's revisions must cite episodes containing other
people's responses; the echo rule refuses self-description grounded only in her
own sends. Her account remains descriptive—what she should do belongs to a
lesson.

Profiles may add typed facts, traits and independently judged guesses under the
[unified profile contract](../unified-memory/profiles.md). That representation
does not relax citation, scope or disclosure requirements.

## Rendering and authority

Declared context-assembly blocks render the room, unit, relevant-person and
Nora accounts required by an occasion. A unit with no account renders no block.
Accounts are bounded as prompt context, but a size limit is not a substitute
for concise synthesis.

Nora's account is the `self` block on room, proactive and Cognition occasions;
it does not replace episodic continuity or commitments held elsewhere. Public
and relevance-gate occasions do not read it. Because the account combines her
acts across a unit, policy hides it where that combined evidence may not be
shown. Lessons rendered beside it inherit the same disclosure decision.

Character is below authored law and does not grant capabilities. It can change
how Nora understands and expresses herself, never who may speak, what may be
disclosed or which actions are admitted.

## Failure behaviour

A revision with unknown citations, copied message identifiers or only
self-citations is refused. An unsettled or failed session retains the previous
head and holds its batch for a bounded retry; it never consumes evidence by
producing an empty answer. A rendering read failure omits the derived block and
is observed without failing the turn.
